On August 7, 2025, Eli Lilly released Phase 3 clinical data for its oral weight loss drug Orforglipron, showing patients achieved an average weight loss of 12.4% after 72 weeks of treatment. This data fell slightly short of Wall Street's expectation of 15%, causing Lilly's stock to drop over 10% in pre-market trading. This result can be seen as a correction following the surge after the June 21 data disclosure, demonstrating the intensifying competition in the GLP-1赛道. The multi-pronged strategy of Lilly and Novo Nordisk in the metabolic field is evident from the 2025 ADA conference data releases:

On June 14 (Saturday), ahead of the ADA annual meeting, Lilly announced Phase 1 data for its Amylin analog Eloralintide—the highest dose group showed an average weight loss of 11.3% at 12 weeks, with favorable gastrointestinal tolerability. The stock pulled back the following week, with market response to Lilly's defensive strategy being cautious.

At the ADA meeting on June 21, Novo Nordisk announced higher-dose Wegovy® (semaglutide 7.2mg, marketed as 诺和盈® in China) STEP UP Phase 3b clinical data, achieving 21% weight loss, with muted market response.

At the ADA meeting on June 21, Lilly released Phase 3 data for oral GLP-1 drug Orforglipron—the high-dose group achieved an average weight reduction of 7.3kg (~16 lbs) at week 40, representing 7.9% of baseline body weight, slightly exceeding expectations.

At the ADA meeting on June 22, Novo Nordisk announced results from the Phase 3 REDEFINE 1 & 2 trials for CagriSema (semaglutide and cagrilintide dual-target combination injection), which showed excellent efficacy but notable gastrointestinal side effects, slightly below expectations.

At the ADA meeting on June 23, Lilly announced that Bimagrumab (ActRIIA/B monoclonal antibody) combined with Novo Nordisk's Wegovy (semaglutide) for 48 weeks resulted in an average weight reduction of 22.1%, with 92.8% of the weight loss coming from fat. The data significantly exceeded expectations, making this therapy clinically significant in obesity treatment.

From an industry analysis perspective, Novo Nordisk is currently employing a predominantly passive defensive strategy, while the market concerns facing Lilly stem more from investor caution about the short-term returns on its diversified布局 rather than product efficacy itself. It should be noted that current market analysis may overestimate the weight loss therapy market potential: Based on long-term observations, considering the heterogeneity of treatment response among patient populations and the treatment adherence challenges posed by gastrointestinal adverse events and psychiatric side effects, market expectations carry the risk of being overly optimistic.

In fact, Lilly adopted an "overlapping mountains" offensive strategy quite early. Orforglipron targets a broader patient population with the convenience and cost-effectiveness of oral administration, while the triple-agonist Retatrutide (GIP/GLP-1/GCG) has achieved significant weight loss progress by simultaneously regulating appetite, promoting insulin secretion, and enhancing energy metabolism efficiency. Clinical data shows the high-dose Retatrutide group achieved an average weight loss of 24.2%, approaching the level of bariatric surgery. From a medical perspective, there is limited clinical necessity to further improve efficacy. Current R&D focus should shift to addressing key challenges facing existing therapies. 2. Think post-GLP1 world in a tightly managed indication.

The original text is quite long, summarized as:

> Complication-specific indications: Design treatment solutions around obesity-related complications (such as MASH, CKD, osteoarthritis) and create separate reimbursement pathways, differentiating from broad-spectrum weight loss drugs.
> Focus on GLP-1 therapy failure populations: Develop second-line therapies for patients who are unresponsive to GLP-1 drugs, experience weight regain, or cannot tolerate gastrointestinal side effects, filling the market gap where approximately 50% of patients discontinue within one year.

On August 15, Novo Nordisk announced that semaglutide received FDA approval for treating adult patients with MASH stage F2-F3, essentially confirming this direction.

Returning to the domestic Chinese market, this round of red ocean competition both replicates the past PD-1 "internal卷" phenomenon and presents new paradigms and opportunities. There is already substantial analytical data proving the market scale of China's diabetic and obese populations. The practical question facing companies is whether they are willing to continue following the me-too approach as followers. To that end, let's first review the actual corporate strategies under the above approaches:

In the development strategy for popular target combinations, obesity-related complications, particularly Metabolic Dysfunction-Associated Steatohepatitis (MASH, formerly non-alcoholic steatohepatitis, renamed in 2023), is a key research direction. Combination therapies at clinical proof-of-concept (POC) stage, including GIPR, GCGR dual-target and triple-target combinations, have multiple companies布局, with Guangzhou Zhongsheng Ruichuang (RAY-1225), Minwei Bio (MWN-101/MWN-109), and Zhongxin Pharmaceutical (ZX2021) leading the pack. Some early pipelines targeting MASH directly integrate FGF21R targeting, based on the 2018 evidence from the first FGF21 analog Pegbelfermin showing hepatic lipid metabolism regulation and anti-fibrotic effects. Pioneers in this field include Dongyangguang Changjiang Pharmaceutical (HEC88473) and Huadong Medicine/Daoer Bio (DR10624). Regarding the necessity of combining FGF21 with GCGR, existing clinical data provides clear evidence. Beyond technical challenges, the core consideration for multi-target strategies is safety assessment, which was demonstrated in the DR10624 clinical data on May 12: 12.5mg, 25mg, 50mg, and 75mg titration groups achieved relative liver fat content reductions of 51.9%, 77.8%, 79.0%, and 75.8% from baseline, respectively. Research shows that designing combination therapies for complications early in the project, based on thorough safety understanding, can effectively optimize target combinations and enhance potential commercial value.

GLP-1 receptor agonists exert their core weight loss mechanism through central appetite suppression. For patient populations not meeting obesity diagnostic criteria, weight rebound after discontinuation is particularly significant. To expand the mass market application and improve clinical benefits, in addition to the ActRIIA/B monoclonal antibody combination therapy, drug development strategies targeting metabolic regulation pathways (such as thyroid hormone receptor β, THRβ) also hold significant value. Related products in development include Gelipharma's ASC47 and HaiChuang Pharmaceutical's HP515.

Regarding ActRIIA/B receptors, Laekna's LAE123 and Bimagrumab's binding affinity for ActRIIA and ActRIIB are worth noting. Animal experiments have already proven that simultaneously targeting ActRIIA/B yields better muscle-building effects than single-target approaches. Whether the affinity differences between dual targets have biological significance remains unclear. Since Bimagrumab (KD values for ActRIIB and ActRIIA are 1.7 pM and 434 pM, respectively) and CDD861 (the novel antibody in the article, KD values for ActRIIB and ActRIIA are 62 pM and 134 pM, respectively), the signal inhibition effects are not significantly different from a practical standpoint. However, as the original text states:

Finally, regarding the convenience of oral small molecule drugs, a topic worth exploring in depth is whether they truly offer advantages over long-acting injectable peptides. In reality, for GLP-1 drugs, patient adherence is typically more affected by gastrointestinal adverse events rather than the administration method itself. This is evidenced by Ascletis Pharma's ASC30 developing both oral and subcutaneous injection routes simultaneously. On August 19, 2025, Viking Therapeutics released topline data from its Phase 2 clinical trial (VENTURE-Oral) for oral GLP-1/GIP dual-target agonist VK2735 for weight loss, further supporting this perspective. Notably, the treatment group in this trial showed significantly higher discontinuation rates than the placebo group, causing the company's stock to plummet 42%. Additionally, by optimizing delivery solutions such as pre-filled injection pens, semaglutide has elevated the convenience of its long-acting peptide drugs to a level comparable to oral formulations (patients can self-administer). Therefore, the choice of administration route is more based on clinical practice rather than absolute advantage. It should also be noted that oral administration may lead to higher missed dose rates and is typically associated with more pronounced gastrointestinal adverse events.

On August 18, Novo Nordisk announced that patients can now purchase semaglutide (Ozempic) through its self-pay pharmacy platform NovoCare at $499 per month, approximately 50% of U.S. market pricing. Amid intensifying market competition, particularly with the trend toward broader adoption of weight loss drugs, pharmaceutical companies are gradually extending competition to sales channel domains. The competition between Lilly and Novo Nordisk has expanded to channel promotion platforms such as NovoCare and Lilly Health. Commerce is important, but it is hoped that the popularization of weight loss drugs can return to the original intention of metabolic health treatment.
