In February 2025, JD.com made a high-profile entry into the food delivery market, launching a "100 Billion Yuan Subsidy" and zero-commission policy, triggering fierce counterattacks from Meituan and Ele.me (Taoxian Spurchase), as the three parties initiated a subsidy war. After multiple regulatory meetings in July, on August 1, the three major platforms issued a joint statement opposing malicious competition, temporarily suspending extreme promotions such as "Zero-Renminbi Purchase."

The competitive landscape of the food delivery industry is primarily determined by its industry characteristics. The food delivery industry has the characteristics of being asset-heavy, operation-heavy, and exhibiting diseconomies of scale—as market share increases, rider costs, algorithm investments, and marketing expenses need to continuously double. After JD.com's high-profile market entry, although it quickly gained market share through short-term subsidy strategies, it faced challenges in continuously optimizing its unit economic model due to incomplete rider networks and merchant resources. Meituan, leveraging its first-mover advantage, established a competitive barrier with 70% market share, with its dedicated riders accounting for 60% and daily orders averaging 60 million, forming significant fulfillment advantages. Ele.me maintained a 20%-30% market share relying on the Alibaba ecosystem. The current market presents a dynamic equilibrium of three-way competition, and JD.com's entry further confirms that the local life service sector, with its long industrial chain, naturally possesses market characteristics of anti-monopoly.

The biopharmaceutical industry chain typically exhibits asset-heavy and operation-heavy characteristics, with diseconomies of scale in the R&D环节, economies of scale in the production环节, and coexistence of economies and diseconomies of scale in the sales环节. The characteristics of the biopharmaceutical industry chain are as follows:

- **R&D Diseconomies of Scale**: Innovative drug R&D requires high investment (over 1 billion yuan per drug), long cycles (10-15 years), and failure rates exceeding 90%, with resource dispersion leading to increasing unit costs. IQVIA data shows that the comprehensive R&D success rate from 2008-2018 did not improve despite increased R&D investment.
- **Production Economies of Scale**: Large-scale production can allocate fixed costs, and the efficiency of specialized equipment with heavy asset investment improves with increased output.
- **Sales Dual Nature**: Economies of scale are reflected in channel reuse (such as covering hospital networks to reduce marginal costs), but innovative drugs require customized promotion (such as oncology drugs requiring professional academic marketing, different from autoimmune diseases), leading to non-linear cost growth.

Through comparative industry data analysis, it is evident that the innovative drug R&D field also conforms to the basic characteristic of being difficult to form long-term market monopoly. Industry statistics show that the change in the share of multinational pharmaceutical companies in innovative drug clinical trial applications (IND) reflects the industry trend of R&D subject decentralization. Among FDA-approved new drugs from global TOP20 MNCs from 2015-2021, only approximately 28% came from internal development, while small and medium-sized biotech companies accounted for 65%.

> This phenomenon stems from the diseconomies of scale in the R&D环节: MNCs' R&D efficiency decreases with scale expansion. Although different MNCs' R&D costs vary greatly, their average cost per project far exceeds that of biotechs (Data from ideapharma): domestic single new drug development expenditure, excluding overseas clinical trials, is estimated at 2-6 billion yuan.

In contrast, from 2020 to 2022, Biotechs, leveraging flexible mechanisms and focusing on niche areas, contributed a significant number of breakthrough therapy designations in cutting-edge directions such as oncology and gene therapy. FDA qualification approval data shows that the subjects receiving designations are primarily small and medium-sized enterprises, with only 19% granted to large MNCs. This distribution characteristic reflects the limited scale effect in early-stage R&D, confirming the structural characteristic that makes it difficult to form R&D centralization in the innovative drug field.

Analysis shows that AI possesses the potential to transform the diseconomies of scale in the R&D stage into economies of scale. This transformation is mainly achieved through engineering means to reduce R&D costs and information means to improve R&D success rates. Accordingly, technical work in the AIDD field should also focus on these two directions to implement precise transformation of the industry. It is worth noting that during the "Golden Ten Years" from 2015 to 2025, the segmented links of the biopharmaceutical industry chain developed rapidly. On one hand, this lowered the operating thresholds for Chinese biotech companies; on the other hand, excessive segmentation of links increased the difficulty of driving overall efficiency improvement through data. Taking the protein design field as an example, although antibody generation technology has significantly reduced antibody discovery costs, it can be predicted that in the next 1-2 years, CRO companies specializing in antibody discovery (such as animal immunization service providers, screening technology service providers, etc.) will collaborate with companies providing antibody generation AI SaaS solutions to continuously optimize cost structures. Given the high sensitivity of data in the biopharmaceutical field, data assets are difficult to flow freely between enterprise entities. This situation may intensify homogeneous competition within the industry while causing AI SaaS companies to face survival pressure due to commercialization difficulties. This situation not only makes it difficult for AI model R&D companies requiring heavy asset investment to maintain technological competitive advantages but also hinders the deep integration process of artificial intelligence and molecular biology technologies.

The author believes that the core distinction between irrational internal competition and rational market competition lies in whether the competitive costs of the downstream of the industrial chain (usually the end-product market) are systematically transferred to the upstream supply chain. Taking the food delivery industry as an example, if the essence of industry competition is through squeezing individual catering merchants and delivery personnel to achieve cost optimization, it constitutes irrational internal competition; conversely, if competition prompts internet platform companies to achieve systematic cost reduction and efficiency improvement by optimizing food delivery algorithms, improving supply-demand matching efficiency, and perfecting logistics infrastructure, it constitutes rational market competition. It should be noted that the latter mode improves overall social welfare through technological innovation, rather than using technological means to intensify malicious competition leading to net social welfare loss.

This core logic also applies to the biopharmaceutical field. However, due to the industry's significant characteristics of long industrial chains and high professional barriers, the complexity of its internal logic far exceeds that of most industries, significantly increasing understanding difficulty. The current highly concerned "centralized procurement" policy in the industry should not simply be attributed to malicious competition caused by policies potentially reducing enterprise profits. Essentially, the policy's core value lies in compressing profit margins of a large number of mature drugs and homogeneous products, prompting market resource reallocation, effectively guiding the industry to avoid low-level repetitive competition, accelerating the formation of differentiated innovation patterns, and promoting the industry toward higher-level innovative development. This policy direction is inherently consistent with the high-quality development strategy of the pharmaceutical industry.

American companies also experienced centralized development in the immune checkpoint inhibitor R&D field, but ultimately formed a relatively balanced market landscape through rapid iteration and differentiated strategic布局. Taking Merck's Keytruda (pembrolizumab), Bristol Myers Squibb's Opdivo (nivolumab), and other PD-1 inhibitors, as well as Roche's Tecentriq (atezolizumab) and other PD-L1 inhibitors as examples, these products not only continuously optimized indication expansion, companion diagnostics, and combination therapy regimens, but also achieved differentiated布局 in areas such as dosing regimens, combination用药 strategies, and biomarker development, effectively avoiding market competitive pressure that might arise from product homogeneity.

The core goal of AIDD (Artificial Intelligence Drug Discovery) through integrating upstream and downstream data is to achieve precise matching of the consumption end and supply end, thereby effectively driving the pharmaceutical industry to solve unmet clinical needs. From a microscopic perspective, AI models for antibody design need to deeply integrate molecular biology technologies to promote molecular-level innovation, achieve differentiated design of same-target drugs, and ultimately lead to the development of best-in-class drugs. From a macroscopic perspective, AI technology should fully leverage its data advantages, reverse diseconomies of scale through multi-link optimization, implement vertical integration to reconstruct upstream and downstream benefit distribution mechanisms, and meet broader unmet clinical needs based on MOA (Mechanism of Action) innovation.
